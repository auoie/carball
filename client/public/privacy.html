Privacy

Carball's offline modes (Free Play, Custom Training, Play vs Bots) need no account. Your settings and training packs stay in this browser's storage, and Free Play, Play vs Bots and your own packs send nothing about you anywhere. Custom Training's shared packs do ask Carball's service: Browse, Enter Code, and playing or saving a shared pack. Like every request to it, these are logged with your IP address (see Logs), and a shared pack's download is counted (see below).

Online play needs an account. This page says what an account stores and how to delete it.

What an account stores

  • The account number of the sign-in you used: Google's or Steam's id for you. Never your e-mail address or your name from Google; we never ask Google for them. A Steam sign-up reads your public Steam name once, only to suggest a display name; it isn't stored.
  • The display name you choose, which other players see. Once your rating in a playlist is no longer provisional, your display name, rating and number of games there also appear on the public leaderboard.
  • Your name changes (the old and new name and when; when we change a name that breaks the rules, or lift a lock on changing it, who did and why), kept for one year for moderation, and deleted with your account.
  • Your ratings, for as long as the account exists, and the results of your ranked matches (who played on which team, who won, who left, how long each played, the ratings before and after), each kept for one year.
  • A queue ban after leaving ranked matches, with its end: each step forgiven after a day without leaving.
  • Your sessions: a cookie that keeps you signed in, for up to 90 days, and a short-lived cookie while a sign-in or sign-up is under way.
  • A ban, if one is ever given, with its reason and end.
  • A lock on changing your name, if one is ever given, with its end: cleared when it ends.
  • The training packs you share: each pack's name, description and shots, its share code and when it was shared, how many times it was downloaded, and your uploads of the last two days (for the daily limit). A shared pack shows your display name publicly as its creator, to anyone who browses or enters its code, until you delete it or a moderator hides it (a hidden pack stays on your list, with why, until you delete it). It is deleted with your account.
  • The shared packs you open while signed in, by day, so each is counted once a day: kept for two days, and deleted with your account.
  • Your reports of other players' shared packs, kept until the pack or your account is deleted.

What it doesn't store

  • E-mail addresses, or the access tokens Google and Steam hand us at sign-in (used once, then dropped).
  • IP addresses, in the account's records. The service holds them in memory to limit how many sign-ins and sign-ups one network can make, and the game server to limit how many connections one network can open. A shared pack's downloads are counted once a day per account or per address: for an address, only a keyed hash of it is kept, for two days.

Logs

The services log, for security, abuse prevention and debugging: IP addresses (with the connection's port, on the game server), account ids and display names (and a rename's new name), and what each connection did: signing in (with Google or Steam), searching (its playlists and regions), joining and leaving a match, a vote to forfeit, its result (each player's team, whether they left, how long they played), a training pack shared (its code and name), deleted or reported (its code), and anything refused. The web server logs each page and file fetched, with the IP address, the browser's user agent and the page it came from. The logs are kept until the servers' log rotation overwrites them (about 100 MB for each service) and are never shared. They never hold your cookies, sign-in codes or tokens.

Others involved

  • Google or Steam, whichever you sign in with, under their own privacy policies.
  • Cloudflare: its Turnstile check runs when you make an account, and its network carries the site's traffic, under Cloudflare's privacy policy.

Deleting your account

Account → Delete Account removes the account at once: its sign-ins, sessions, ratings and shared training packs. Ranked results you played in stay for the other players, without your account in them. Lines the logs already hold (its id and names among them) stay until the rotation overwrites them. A keyed hash of the sign-in's id is kept for 30 days, so that the same sign-in can't make a new account in that time, then removed; a ban stays with the sign-in it was given to.
